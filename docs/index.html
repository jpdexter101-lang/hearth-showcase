Meet Ember

An administrator, not just a chatbot

Ember is Hearth's AI. It doesn't stop at answering questions: it looks into what's really happening on your machines, explains it in plain words, and fixes things through plans you approve. It never gets a shell, your files, or your passwords, and anything that changes your homelab waits for your yes.

You can rename Ember and give it a different voice and look. Ember thinks with the AI service you connect.

Development updates

Recent progress

  1. October 30, 2026 · Interface

    A new Home: Ember in the middle, everything on one screen

    Home now fits on one screen. Ember sits in the middle with your active projects around it, what needs you is a row of small buttons along the top, and your homelab's vital signs sit below.

    Ember's chat window now stays open as you move between pages, so you can keep talking while you open a setting or a service. And any service that is down has an Ask Ember why button.

    Built and tested; in review.

  2. October 30, 2026 · Integrations

    Clear out dead torrents properly

    A stalled torrent with no seeders can now be removed from qBittorrent together with the data it left behind. Like every change, it is a plan you approve first, and the plan says plainly that the deletion is permanent.

    Built and tested; in review.

  3. October 28, 2026 · Storage

    Storage and RAID you can understand

    The Storage page shows every drive's health and temperature and explains your arrays in plain words: mdadm, ZFS, Btrfs, SnapRAID, and Windows Storage Spaces. When a disk fails, it tells you what that means and what to do next.

    Built and tested; in review.

  4. October 27, 2026 · AI

    Bring your own AI: Claude Code, Codex, or a local model

    Ember can now run on Claude Code, Codex, or a model on your own hardware through Ollama. Whichever you pick gets the same tools, the same memory of your homelab, and the same approval rules.

    The War Room is where you work with it on bigger jobs, with your projects from every PC in one place.

    Built and tested; in review.

  5. October 26, 2026 · Voice

    Talk to Ember out loud

    Ember can speak with a built-in voice that needs no setup, and you can dictate instead of typing. If you have a spare machine with a graphics card, Hearth can set it up as a speech machine for more natural voices.

    Built and tested; in review.

  6. October 23, 2026 · Multi-PC

    One account across all your PCs, on Linux and Windows

    Hearth runs on each PC you own, Linux or Windows, and they join together under one account with a pairing code. Each PC watches its own services, and you see and manage all of them from any of them, or from your phone.

    Updates are offered, never forced: Hearth tells you a new version is out and you update when you are ready.

    Built and tested; in review.

  7. October 17, 2026 · Onboarding

    Connect your AI, right after you create your account

    Setup now goes straight from creating your account to connecting Ember's AI. Hearth only offers the services it can really connect to, explains in plain English what AI use costs, and runs a three-part test before Ember uses it: is it installed, is it signed in and answering, and can Ember use Hearth's tools through it.

    You can skip it, and everything that doesn't need an AI keeps working. Close the browser halfway and setup picks up where you left off.

    Built and tested; in review.

  8. October 17, 2026 · Roadmap

    Where we're headed: Ember builds your homelab

    We've written down the whole first-run experience we're building toward: tell Ember what you'd love to do, let it look at your real setup, approve a plan, then watch it create accounts, store keys safely, configure each app, and check that everything works.

    It will be built in small steps, starting with apps you already run, and every change keeps needing your approval.

    Planned.

  9. October 16, 2026 · Planning

    Plans that never quietly drop what you asked for

    The setup planner now keeps every wish you state. Anything it can't fit on your computer is shown with the reason, what it would take, and other ways to get it, and you choose whether to include it anyway, save it for later, or accept a substitute.

    Built and tested; in review.

  10. October 16, 2026 · Identity

    Hello, Hearth. Meet Ember.

    The project has its name and its look: Hearth, with an ember-orange theme, and Ember, its AI administrator, shown as a living flame. You can rename Ember, choose from five voices and twenty-five looks, or use your own picture.

    Built and tested; in review.

  11. October 15, 2026 · Integrations

    Requested movies, followed until they arrive

    Hearth can follow the movies you request, notice when their digital release date arrives, and tell you once if something is in the way. It only reads: it never searches or downloads on its own.

    Built and tested; in review.
