Meet Ember

An administrator, not just a chatbot

Ember is Hearth's AI. It doesn't stop at answering questions: it looks into what's really happening on your machines, explains it in plain words, and fixes things through plans you approve. It never gets a shell, your files, or your passwords, and anything that changes your homelab waits for your yes.

You can rename Ember and give it a different voice and look. Ember thinks with the AI service you connect.

Ten looks to pick from. Ember's name and voice are yours to change too.

Free and paid

Free for your home, with a few paid add-ons

Hearth is free for personal and home use. Two optional add-ons will cost extra, and businesses need a licence. We're saying so now, before anyone downloads it.

Free

  • Everything a single Hearth does: Ember, a panel for each app, monitoring, the vault, plans you approve, backups and undo.
  • As many phones, tablets and wall screens as you like, paired to your Hearth.
  • As many separate Hearths as you like, one on each PC.
  • Your own AI: Claude Code, Codex, or a local model through Ollama, at no cost from us.

Paid add-ons

  • Hearth Connected: Link your Hearths on different PCs so they work as one, with every PC's projects and systems in one place. Paid from the first link.
  • Show & Tell: While Hearth's AI works, it opens and points at what it is examining, and Teach me explains why it matters. The AI does the same work without it.

Businesses, consultants, and anyone using Hearth to make money will need a business licence.

Prices aren't set yet. Once a feature ships free, it never moves behind a payment.

Development updates

Recent progress

  1. November 6, 2026 · Home

    A home dashboard for the wall, the phone and the TV

    Hearth now turns Home Assistant into a full-screen home dashboard. The time is written out in words, the window shows the real weather outside, and the cards cover what's playing, today's calendar, shopping lists, rooms, doors and scenes.

    Music shows the song's poster, can play its official music video in the card, and can send it to a TV. Scenes are made and edited right on the dashboard, and opening a door asks for a PIN.

    A paired wall tablet sees only the home dashboard, nothing else of Hearth.

    Built and tested; in review.

  2. October 30, 2026 · Friends and family

    Watch party invitations get a banner

    Pick a style for each watch party invitation. The new Banner style draws one picture like a streaming app's: the title, the time and your note over the movie's own backdrop, with its poster beside them. Discord shows it at the top of the post, with the details and the I'm in and Can't make it buttons below.

    Cinematic, Streaming, and Compact are there too, and you can change the style later; the post on Discord updates itself. Shown here with Night of the Living Dead (1968, public domain).

    Released in a development build (0.40.6); waiting for a first real-world post.

  3. October 30, 2026 · Interface

    A new Home: Ember in the middle, everything on one screen

    Home now fits on one screen. Ember sits in the middle with your active projects around it, what needs you is a row of small buttons along the top, and your homelab's vital signs sit below.

    Ember's chat window now stays open as you move between pages, so you can keep talking while you open a setting or a service. And any service that is down has an Ask Ember why button.

    Built and tested; in review.

  4. October 30, 2026 · Integrations

    Clear out dead torrents properly

    A stalled torrent with no seeders can now be removed from qBittorrent together with the data it left behind. Like every change, it is a plan you approve first, and the plan says plainly that the deletion is permanent.

    Built and tested; in review.

  5. October 28, 2026 · Storage

    Storage and RAID you can understand

    The Storage page shows every drive's health and temperature and explains your arrays in plain words: mdadm, ZFS, Btrfs, SnapRAID, and Windows Storage Spaces. When a disk fails, it tells you what that means and what to do next.

    Built and tested; in review.

  6. October 28, 2026 · Friends and family

    Movie nights: plan a watch party in Hearth

    Pick a movie from your library and a time, add a note and where to watch (Watch Together in the Plezy app, Jellyfin SyncPlay, or come over), and Hearth posts the invitation to your friends, with reminders before it starts.

    Posted by a Discord bot, the invitation has I'm in and Can't make it buttons, and the guest list keeps itself. Change the time or add a room code and everyone gets the update; cancel it and they hear that too.

    Built and tested; in review.

  7. October 28, 2026 · Friends and family

    Tell your friends when something new arrives

    When a movie or show becomes available in Plex or Jellyfin, not just downloaded, Hearth can announce it to a friends and family channel with its poster, details, and trailer. You design the post and see exactly how Discord will show it.

    What is already in your library is never announced, and a test message always says it is a test. Add Discord in two clicks with the official Hearth bot, or set up your own bot step by step.

    Built and tested; in review. The official Hearth bot is waiting on its first real-world test.

  8. October 27, 2026 · AI

    Bring your own AI: Claude Code, Codex, or a local model

    Ember can now run on Claude Code, Codex, or a model on your own hardware through Ollama. Whichever you pick gets the same tools, the same memory of your homelab, and the same approval rules.

    The War Room is where you work with it on bigger jobs. With Hearth Connected, a paid add-on, it shows your projects from every PC in one place.

    Built and tested; in review.

  9. October 26, 2026 · Voice

    Talk to Ember out loud

    Ember can speak with a built-in voice that needs no setup, and you can dictate instead of typing. If you have a spare machine with a graphics card, Hearth can set it up as a speech machine for more natural voices.

    Built and tested; in review.

  10. October 23, 2026 · Multi-PC

    One account across all your PCs, on Linux and Windows

    Hearth runs on each PC you own, Linux or Windows, and they join together under one account with a pairing code. Each PC watches its own services, and you see and manage all of them from any of them, or from your phone.

    Update, November 6: linking your PCs this way is Hearth Connected, a paid add-on. Hearth on each PC on its own stays free.

    Updates are offered, never forced: Hearth tells you a new version is out and you update when you are ready.

    Built and tested; in review.

  11. October 19, 2026 · Administration

    Ember can install new apps, not just fix them

    Ember can now add a new app to your homelab as its own Docker Compose project, update an app to a newer version, or remove one while keeping its files and data. Each is a plan you approve first.

    Hearth checks every change afterwards, on the containers and on the app itself, and puts things back on its own if the check fails. Building a whole homelab from your starter plan in one guided run is the next step.

    Built and tested; in review.

  12. October 17, 2026 · Onboarding

    Connect your AI, right after you create your account

    Setup now goes straight from creating your account to connecting Ember's AI. Hearth only offers the services it can really connect to, explains in plain English what AI use costs, and runs a three-part test before Ember uses it: is it installed, is it signed in and answering, and can Ember use Hearth's tools through it.

    You can skip it, and everything that doesn't need an AI keeps working. Close the browser halfway and setup picks up where you left off.

    Built and tested; in review.

  13. October 17, 2026 · Roadmap

    Where we're headed: Ember builds your homelab

    We've written down the whole first-run experience we're building toward: tell Ember what you'd love to do, let it look at your real setup, approve a plan, then watch it create accounts, store keys safely, configure each app, and check that everything works.

    It will be built in small steps, starting with apps you already run, and every change keeps needing your approval.

    Planned.

  14. October 16, 2026 · Planning

    Plans that never quietly drop what you asked for

    The setup planner now keeps every wish you state. Anything it can't fit on your computer is shown with the reason, what it would take, and other ways to get it, and you choose whether to include it anyway, save it for later, or accept a substitute.

    Built and tested; in review.

  15. October 16, 2026 · Identity

    Hello, Hearth. Meet Ember.

    The project has its name and its look: Hearth, with an ember-orange theme, and Ember, its AI administrator, shown as a living flame. You can rename Ember, choose from five voices and twenty-five looks, or use your own picture.

    Built and tested; in review.

  16. October 15, 2026 · Integrations

    Requested movies, followed until they arrive

    Hearth can follow the movies you request, notice when their digital release date arrives, and tell you once if something is in the way. It only reads: it never searches or downloads on its own.

    Built and tested; in review.
